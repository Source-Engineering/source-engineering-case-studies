# Qualitative data for the 2026 legal-summarization case study

> Illustrative data. The protocol is pre-registered but the experiment has not been run. The themes below are a worked example of the case-study surface.

## Themes from the COTS-on condition

### Trust in the summary

> "I trust the summary more because I can see the evidence. If the evidence is there, I don't have to re-read the document. If the evidence is not there, I know the summary is unsupported."

### The COTS as a review aid

> "The COTS is like a second pair of eyes. I can challenge the summary by looking at the evidence. Without the COTS, I had to take the summary on faith."

### When the COTS did not help

> "When the evidence was the model's own reasoning, the COTS didn't help. I still didn't know if the model got it right; the COTS just confirmed the model had reasoned about it."

## Themes from the COTS-off condition

### Hidden reasoning

> "Without the COTS, I had to re-read the document myself. The summary told me what the model thought, but not why."

### Latency trade-off

> "I played it safe. If the system could have given me the evidence, I would have asked. But it didn't, so I just rejected the summary and did it myself."

## Cross-condition themes

### The user's role

> "The COTS changed my role. I went from being the model's editor to being the model's reviewer. The reviewer checks the model's work; the editor rewrites it."

### The judgment gap

> "The COTS didn't close the judgment gap. It closed the hesitation gap. I still have to decide what to do; the COTS just made the decision auditable."
